The landscape of dev and AI learning resources is vast, noisy, and changes fast. This page is a curated shortlist — not a comprehensive index, but a starting point of resources that consistently deliver signal over noise. Whether you're building foundational ML knowledge from scratch, leveling up your systems design, or staying current with the AI field, these are the places worth spending time.

---

## AI and Machine Learning

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="fast.ai — Practical Deep Learning" icon="graduation-cap" href="https://fast.ai">
    **What it is:** A free course by Jeremy Howard that teaches deep learning top-down — you build working models in week one, then learn the theory underneath.

    **Why it's great:** The opposite of most ML courses. You get intuition from practice before drowning in math. Covers PyTorch, computer vision, NLP, and tabular data.

    **Best for:** Developers who learn by doing and want practical ML skills fast.

    **Format:** Free online course + Jupyter notebooks.
  </Card>

  <Card title="DeepLearning.AI" icon="brain" href="https://deeplearning.ai">
    **What it is:** Andrew Ng's learning platform with the original Machine Learning Specialization and Deep Learning Specialization on Coursera, plus newer short courses on LLMs, LangChain, and MLOps.

    **Why it's great:** Best structured, rigorous curriculum for ML fundamentals. Andrew Ng's explanations are legendarily clear.

    **Best for:** Building solid theoretical foundations alongside practical skills.

    **Format:** Coursera (paid with audit option) + free short courses on deeplearning.ai.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Andrej Karpathy's YouTube" icon="youtube" href="https://youtube.com/@AndrejKarpathy">
    **What it is:** The former Tesla AI Director and OpenAI co-founder building neural networks from scratch on camera — GPT, backprop, tokenizers.

    **Why it's great:** Watching an expert build a mini-GPT from a blank file demystifies the entire transformer architecture. "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero" series is essential viewing.

    **Best for:** Developers who want to understand LLMs at the implementation level, not just use them.

    **Format:** Free YouTube videos (2–4 hours each).
  </Card>

  <Card title="Papers With Code" icon="file-code" href="https://paperswithcode.com">
    **What it is:** A free resource that links ML research papers with their open-source implementations and benchmark leaderboards.

    **Why it's great:** Every major model and technique has a paper here, linked to the code. Great for going from "I heard about X" to "here's the paper and here's how to run it."

    **Best for:** Staying current with the research frontier; finding implementations to experiment with.

    **Format:** Website with searchable papers, code repos, and state-of-the-art benchmarks.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Accordion title="More ML and AI resources">

**Hugging Face Course** — Free course on transformers, fine-tuning, and the Hugging Face ecosystem. Best hands-on introduction to working with open-source models. [huggingface.co/learn](https://huggingface.co/learn)

**Full Stack Deep Learning** — Course focused on the production side of ML: data pipelines, training infrastructure, deployment, monitoring. [fullstackdeeplearning.com](https://fullstackdeeplearning.com)

**Anthropic's Courses** — Short, focused courses on prompt engineering and Claude-specific features. Free. [anthropic.com/education](https://www.anthropic.com/education)

**The Illustrated Transformer (Jay Alammar)** — The best visual explanation of the transformer architecture. A single blog post that's referenced more than most textbooks. [jalammar.github.io](https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-transformer/)

**CS231n (Stanford)** — Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition. Free lecture videos and notes from Stanford. Still one of the best deep learning courses for computer vision.

</Accordion>

## Prompt Engineering

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="DAIR.AI Prompt Engineering Guide" icon="book" href="https://promptingguide.ai">
    **What it is:** An open-source, community-maintained guide covering all major prompting techniques: zero-shot, few-shot, CoT, RAG, agents, and more.

    **Why it's great:** Dense, well-organized, and updated regularly as new techniques emerge. Includes research citations for each technique and code examples.

    **Best for:** Getting a solid theoretical grounding in prompting techniques.
  </Card>

  <Card title="LearnPrompting.org" icon="lightbulb" href="https://learnprompting.org">
    **What it is:** A free, open-source course on prompt engineering with a focus on practical application for developers and non-developers alike.

    **Why it's great:** More accessible and example-heavy than the DAIR guide. Good for beginners and for teams you're trying to bring up to speed on AI tools.

    **Best for:** Beginners; training non-technical teammates on effective AI use.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Accordion title="Advanced prompting papers and resources">

**Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models** — The original Google Research paper that introduced CoT. [arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903)

**ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models** — Foundational paper for AI agents that reason before acting. Behind most modern agent frameworks. [arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)

**Anthropic Prompt Library** — A curated collection of production-ready Claude prompts for common tasks. Useful for seeing how to structure complex prompts. [console.anthropic.com/library](https://console.anthropic.com/library)

</Accordion>

## Software Development

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Refactoring Guru" icon="recycle" href="https://refactoring.guru">
    **What it is:** The best visual reference for design patterns and refactoring techniques. Covers all 23 GoF patterns with clear diagrams, code examples in multiple languages, and real-world analogies.

    **Why it's great:** Patterns are explained with visuals and concrete code, not abstract UML-heavy theory. When you see a pattern name in a PR review or architecture doc, this is where you look it up.

    **Best for:** Design patterns, refactoring techniques, code structure.
  </Card>

  <Card title="roadmap.sh" icon="map" href="https://roadmap.sh">
    **What it is:** Community-maintained visual learning roadmaps for Frontend, Backend, DevOps, System Design, and more.

    **Why it's great:** Answers the perennial question "what should I learn next?" with a structured, community-vetted map. Also links to recommended resources for each topic.

    **Best for:** Planning your learning path, identifying skill gaps.
  </Card>

  <Card title="The Pragmatic Programmer" icon="wrench">
    **What it is:** The classic software craftsmanship book by David Thomas and Andrew Hunt. Covers career advice, thinking tools, and coding practices that age well.

    **Why it's great:** Still the best single book on becoming a better developer in the broad sense — not any specific language or framework. Covers DRY, orthogonality, debugging mindset, and dozens of other fundamentals.

    **Best for:** Junior to mid-level developers; career mindset.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Clean Code" icon="sparkles">
    **What it is:** Robert C. Martin's guide to writing readable, maintainable code. Covers naming, functions, comments, formatting, error handling.

    **Why it's great:** Gives you a shared vocabulary for code quality discussions. Even if you disagree with some of its prescriptions, it forces you to think deliberately about code structure.

    **Best for:** Improving daily coding habits; code review culture.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Accordion title="Forums and Link Aggregators">

**Hacker News (news.ycombinator.com)** — The gold standard for tech news discussion. Comments on AI papers and launches often contain significant expert insight. The "Show HN" section surfaces interesting open-source projects.

**Dev.to** — Blogging platform and community for developers. Useful for practical tutorials and career content. Less noise than Medium.

**Lobste.rs** — Invite-only link aggregator like Hacker News but with stricter focus on computing topics and higher quality discussions.

## Recommended Books

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Designing Data-Intensive Applications" icon="database">
    **Author:** Martin Kleppmann  
    **Level:** Mid–Senior

    The best book on databases, distributed systems, and backend architecture. Covers replication, sharding, consistency, consensus, stream processing, and more — explained clearly with real-world context. Known as "DDIA". Essential for backend/infra engineers.
  </Card>

  <Card title="System Design Interview" icon="sitemap">
    **Author:** Alex Xu  
    **Level:** Mid–Senior

    Practical introduction to system design concepts through worked examples: URL shorteners, social feeds, notification systems, distributed caches. Volumes 1 and 2 together cover most interview scenarios and also serve as a practical reference for real architectural decisions.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Clean Code" icon="sparkles">
    **Author:** Robert C. Martin  
    **Level:** Junior–Mid

    The definitive guide to writing readable, maintainable code. Covers naming conventions, function design, comments, formatting, and error handling. Required reading for anyone who cares about code quality. Read it once, then keep it as a reference when doing code reviews.
  </Card>

  <Card title="The Pragmatic Programmer" icon="wrench">
    **Author:** David Thomas & Andrew Hunt  
    **Level:** All levels

    The most timeless book in software engineering — doesn't teach a specific language or framework, but teaches you how to think about your craft. DRY, orthogonality, tracer bullets, programming by coincidence. Read the 20th anniversary edition for updated examples.
  </Card>

  <Card title="A Philosophy of Software Design" icon="compass">
    **Author:** John Ousterhout  
    **Level:** Mid–Senior

    A more nuanced counterpoint to Clean Code. Focuses on managing complexity through deep modules, strategic vs tactical programming, and when comments are actually valuable. Shorter and more thought-provoking than most design books.
  </Card>

  <Card title="The Staff Engineer's Path" icon="road">
    **Author:** Tanya Reilly  
    **Level:** Senior+

    The definitive guide to the Staff Engineer role and beyond. Covers technical strategy, organizational influence, project leadership without authority, and navigating the IC track. Essential reading if you're approaching or working at the senior/staff level.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
